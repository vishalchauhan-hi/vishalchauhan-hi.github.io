Your CV says what you have done. Your cover letter has a different job: it argues why you are the right person for **this** position, at **this** lab or company, right now. A committee often reads dozens of letters for one opening, and many decide in the first paragraph whether to read the rest carefully.

So the question to answer in every line is simple: *why you, why here, why now?*

## The anatomy of a good letter

A strong research cover letter has five parts. Each has one job.

## Length and tone

- **One page** for postdoc and industry roles; up to two for faculty applications if the call asks for it.
- Roughly **350–600 words**. White space is your friend.
- Write like a confident colleague, not a supplicant. Prefer *“I built”*, *“we showed”* over *“I was involved in”*.
- Use the same words as the job advertisement where they are true for you. Readers (and sometimes software) scan for them.

## Tailor three things, every time

A generic letter is easy to spot. You do not need to rewrite everything for each application, but you should always change these three:

1. **The name and the role.** The person or committee, and the exact title of the position.
2. **Paragraph 3.** Their specific projects, papers or products, and how your work connects.
3. **The emphasis in paragraph 2.** Lead with the result that matters most *to them*.

<div class="callout"><p><b>Tip:</b> keep a master letter with your strongest paragraphs, and a short file of notes per application (who, what they work on, which of your results fits). Tailoring then takes an hour, not a day.</p></div>

## Common mistakes

- Starting with *“My name is…”*. Your name is already at the top.
- Listing everything from your CV instead of choosing what matters for this role.
- Talking only about what *you* want (“this would be a great opportunity for me”) and not what you will bring.
- Spelling the person’s name, the lab or the company wrong. Check it twice.
- Sending a Word file when a PDF was requested, or a file named `letter_final_v3.pdf`. Use `Lastname_CoverLetter_Lab.pdf`.

## A skeleton to start from

```
Dear [Name / Hiring committee],

I am applying for the [exact position] at [lab / company]. I am a
[current role] at [institution], where I [one-line pitch].

[Why you fit: 2–3 concrete results that match their needs, with evidence.]

[What you will bring here: connect your next steps to their work by name.]

[Beyond research: teaching, mentoring, engineering or collaboration,
with one example each.]

I am available from [date]. My CV and [other documents] are attached.
Thank you for your time and consideration.

Sincerely,
[Name]
```
